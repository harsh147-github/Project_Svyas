## Part 1: Understanding AIKosh and the IndiaAI ecosystem

### AIKosh is India's national AI data repository, not a compute platform

AIKosh (aikosh.indiaai.gov.in), launched **March 6, 2025** by MeitY Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw, is the datasets pillar of the ₹10,371.92 crore IndiaAI Mission. It serves as a centralized, free repository of **non-personal, anonymized, India-specific AI artefacts** — datasets, pre-trained models, use cases, and an AI sandbox IDE. As of February 2026, AIKosh hosts **7,541 datasets and 273 AI models** across 20 sectors, with over 3.85 lakh visits, 11,000 registered users, and 26,000 downloads. It remains in beta.

Critical distinction: AIKosh provides *data and models*, not compute. The GPU infrastructure lives at the **IndiaAI Compute Portal** (compute.indiaai.gov.in) — a separate platform under the same mission. Sushaasan needs to access *both*: AIKosh for governance datasets and sovereign language models (Sarvam AI models, BharatGen, Bhashini speech data), and IndiaAI Compute for the GPU-hours to run its 6-stage NLP pipeline.

The relationship between the three key platforms is straightforward. **AIKosh** = data layer (₹199.55 crore budget, free access, 7,541 datasets). **IndiaAI Compute** = infrastructure layer (₹4,563 crore budget, 38,231 GPUs at subsidized rates, up to 40% subsidy). **Bhashini** = language technology layer (separate MeitY initiative for 22 Indian languages, with 350+ models; some models cross-listed on AIKosh). Models developed under the IndiaAI Innovation Centre — including Sarvam-1, Sarvam-30B, Sarvam-105B, and BharatGen — are uploaded to AIKosh for developer access, and can be fine-tuned using IndiaAI Compute GPUs.

### What AIKosh makes available for Sushaasan specifically

AIKosh's datasets span governance, language, and socioeconomic domains directly relevant to civic intelligence. The **Bhashini speech recognition dataset** (1,684 hours of labeled speech across 12 Indian languages) is invaluable for voice-based civic input processing. **Census 2011 village-level geometries**, Economic Census data, Kisan Call Center query datasets, and the Telangana Open Data Initiative provide governance ground truth. **AI4Bharat language detection models** (23 languages) and Sarvam's multilingual LLMs — including the free Sarvam-30B and Sarvam-105B released under Apache License on AIKosh in March 2026 — can power Sushaasan's translation and NER pipeline. BharatGen, India's first government-funded multimodal LLM supporting 22 languages (₹900 crore project, led by IIT Bombay), is also available on the platform.

Access is free. Register via DigiLocker (individuals) or MeitY's Entity Locker (organizations). No monetization is planned — the government confirmed this in a Rajya Sabha response.

### The IndiaAI Mission's seven pillars and budget allocation

The IndiaAI Mission, approved by the Union Cabinet on March 7, 2024, allocates its ₹10,371.92 crore across seven pillars. **IndiaAI Compute** receives the largest share at **₹4,563.36 crore**, followed by **IndiaAI Innovation Centre** (foundation models) at **₹1,971.37 crore**, **IndiaAI Startup Financing** at **₹1,942.50 crore**, **IndiaAI Application Development Initiative** at **₹689.05 crore**, **IndiaAI FutureSkills** at **₹551.69 crore**, AIKosh at ₹199.55 crore, and Safe & Trusted AI at ₹20.46 crore.

A critical reality check: per Medianama's April 2026 analysis, **only ~₹400 crore has been released in two years** against the five-year outlay. The FY 2025-26 revised expenditure estimate was reduced from ₹2,000 crore to ₹800 crore. Over 85% of subsidy disbursements have gone toward indigenous generative AI model development. This means the window for compute subsidy access may actually be *less* competitive than expected — the funds exist but are being deployed cautiously. The FY 2026-27 budget allocates ~₹1,000 crore, and the government is reportedly considering doubling the total corpus to ~₹20,000 crore.

## Part 2: Eligibility, application process, and timeline

### Sushaasan qualifies under the early-stage startup category

The IndiaAI Compute Portal's End-User Policy (February 2025) defines six eligibility categories. Sushaasan's profile — DPIIT-recognized, pre-revenue, pre-seed, solo student founder — maps to **Category 3.6: Early-stage startups** (pre-revenue/funding, requiring 4+ years founder experience in AI, with endorsement by CEO IndiaAI or Joint Secretary-rank officer). However, given the founder's student status, the **Category 3.5: Students** pathway (requiring APAAR ID, student profile, and faculty endorsement) may be more immediately accessible.

The standard startup category (Category 3.4) requires annual revenue of ₹50–200 lakhs OR funding of ≥₹100 lakhs plus demonstrated AI/ML experience — criteria Sushaasan does not yet meet. The recommended strategy is to apply under the **student category** for initial access (lower barrier, auto-approval possible for <5,000 GPU hours), then transition to the startup category as Sushaasan scales.

Required documents for application:

- DPIIT Recognition Certificate (Startup India BHASKAR ID: IN-0326-9427IW)
- Certificate of Incorporation
- APAAR ID (for student pathway)
- Faculty endorsement letter (from college/university)
- Detailed project proposal (Annexure I format): title, description, technical approach, envisioned impact, industry domain, team details, funding source
- Estimated Bill of Materials: GPU hours, GPU count, services needed, subsidy percentage requested

### Step-by-step application process

**Step 1: Register** on the IndiaAI Compute Portal using Meri Pehchaan credentials (DigiLocker/Parichay/ePramaan). Verify via OTP. **Step 2: Submit eligibility documents** — DPIIT certificate plus student credentials. Documents are verified electronically. **Step 3: Submit project proposal** in Annexure I format, including a detailed Bill of Materials specifying GPU hours, GPU type, and subsidy request. **Step 4: Review and approval** — requests for ≤5,000 GPU-hours and ≤50 GPUs without subsidy can be auto-approved; larger requests or subsidy applications go to the **PMEC (Program Monitoring and Evaluation Committee)**, which meets twice monthly. Submit by the 25th of the month; approved list published by the 10th of the following month. **Step 5: Access compute** through empaneled service providers (Yotta, E2E Networks, Tata Communications, Jio Platforms, and others) within 30 calendar days of approval.

The PMEC evaluates projects on clarity, uniqueness, national importance (impacts 10 lakh+ citizens or ₹500 crore+ economic value), alignment with SDGs/social good, team experience, and past performance. **Sushaasan's civic governance focus and multilingual inclusion angle directly map to the "national importance" and SDG criteria.**

## Part 4: Technical compute requirements and financial analysis

### GPU needs scale from ₹776/month to ₹43.4 lakh/month across four phases

Sushaasan's 6-job BullMQ pipeline — language detection/translation, topic clustering, NER extraction, sentiment/urgency scoring, consensus detection, and summary generation — has well-defined compute requirements at each scale. Using a hybrid model (Sarvam's free LLM APIs for NER/sentiment/summary + AIKosh GPUs for embeddings and clustering) dramatically reduces costs versus full self-hosted GPU inference.

| Scale | Daily Active Users | Messages/Day | GPU-hrs/Month | Hybrid Monthly Cost |
|-------|-------------------|-------------|---------------|-------------------|
| **MVP** (1K users) | 150 | 1,500 | 3 | **₹776** ($9) |
| **City** (50K users) | 7,500 | 75,000 | 100 | **₹34,200** ($407) |
| **Multi-city** (500K users) | 75,000 | 750,000 | 1,000 | **₹3,42,000** ($4,071) |
| **National** (10M users) | 1,500,000 | 15,000,000 | 20,000 | **₹43,40,000** ($51,667) |

The cost advantage of AIKosh is substantial. **IndiaAI's H100 GPUs at ₹92/hour are 3.6× cheaper than AWS (₹328/hour post-June 2025 price cuts) and 6.4× cheaper than Azure (₹586/hour).** Even against Indian providers like E2E Networks (₹249/hour), IndiaAI delivers a 63% cost reduction. With the 40% subsidy, the effective rate drops further. A critical insight: **Sarvam AI's 30B and 105B LLMs are currently free per token**, meaning Sushaasan can offload 50-70% of its GPU-intensive inference (NER, sentiment, summary generation) to Sarvam's API at zero compute cost, reserving AIKosh GPUs solely for embedding generation, clustering, and vector similarity operations.

Over a phased 12-month deployment (3 months development → 3 months college pilot → 3 months city expansion → 3 months multi-city), the total cost varies dramatically by infrastructure choice:

## Part 5: Strategic alignment with government priorities

### Sushaasan maps directly onto India's AI-for-governance agenda

The platform aligns with multiple active government priorities in ways that strengthen the AIKosh pitch. The **"AI for Viksit Bharat 2047"** vision explicitly calls for AI applications that improve governance quality and citizen participation. The IndiaAI Application Development Initiative lists "Improved Governance through the use of language technologies" as a theme — Sushaasan is essentially a purpose-built solution for this exact brief.

**The CPGRAMS gap creates a clear opportunity.** CPGRAMS processes ~20 lakh grievances annually but reaches only **0.19% of India's population**. IIT Kanpur's IGMS 2.0 (launched October 2023, MoU signed December 2021) modernized the *official-facing* analytics dashboard — reducing average grievance disposal time from 32 days to 13 days. But IGMS 2.0 is an analytics tool for bureaucrats, not a citizen participation platform. Sushaasan occupies the **citizen-facing participatory layer** that IGMS 2.0 lacks, capturing the **500+ million Indians discussing governance on social media** who never file formal grievances. The two systems are complementary, not competitive.

The **Digital Public Infrastructure (India Stack)** philosophy — building shared, open, interoperable infrastructure — is Sushaasan's architectural DNA. Just as UPI created a unified payment layer, Aadhaar a unified identity layer, and DigiLocker a unified document layer, Sushaasan creates a **unified civic intelligence layer**. The sovereignty argument resonates powerfully: civic governance data — what citizens think about roads, water, education, healthcare — processed on Indian infrastructure using Indian AI models (Sarvam, Bhashini, BharatGen) represents a **strategic national asset**. This data currently flows through American platforms (Twitter/X, Meta, Google) with no structured capture for governance use.

### Five alignment narratives for the pitch

**Multilingual inclusion**: Bhashini integration means Sushaasan processes civic discourse in all 22 scheduled languages, ensuring Tier-2/3 cities and rural voices are not excluded by language barriers. This aligns with the government's Bhashini mission and the broader "AI for All" agenda. **Smart Cities transition**: As the Smart Cities Mission evolves into City Economic Regions, AI-powered civic intelligence becomes essential infrastructure for evidence-based urban governance. The Centre of Excellence in AI for Sustainable Cities at IIT Kanpur directly validates this use case. **Data sovereignty**: India currently has no structured multilingual civic discourse dataset. Sushaasan generates one — processable on Indian infrastructure, contributing back to AIKosh, and training future Indian civic AI models. **Participatory, not surveillance AI**: Sushaasan aggregates *publicly shared* civic opinions (WhatsApp forwards, Twitter threads, Reddit discussions, Instagram stories) — it does not monitor private communications. The framing is "digital gram sabha," not surveillance. **Government efficiency augmentation**: Sushaasan helps officials understand citizen priorities *before* they become formal grievances, enabling proactive governance rather than reactive complaint resolution.

## Part 6: Comparable success stories and lessons

### Sarvam AI's playbook is the model to follow

Sarvam AI's trajectory from AI4Bharat research lab to ₹246.72 crore government contract offers the clearest template. Founded August 2023 by Vivek Raghavan and Pratyush Kumar (both from IIT Madras), Sarvam was selected from 67 applicants to build India's sovereign LLM, receiving 4,000 GPUs for 6 months via Yotta, Tata Communications, and E2E Networks. Their playbook: **sovereign narrative first** ("India's own AI, not Silicon Valley's"), **volunteer/pro-bono government work to build credibility** (the UIDAI/Aadhaar partnership was through UIDAI's volunteer policy with no explicit payment), **open-source releases to build ecosystem trust** (Apache License 2.0 on Hugging Face and AIKosh), and **IIT institutional backing** for government legitimacy.

Sushaasan should replicate key elements: position as sovereign civic AI infrastructure (not a commercial startup), offer a free pilot to a government body (district administration, Smart City SPV, or DARPG), plan to open-source the civic discourse dataset on AIKosh, and seek academic endorsement from an IIT or NIT faculty member working on governance/NLP.

### The civic tech ecosystem validates the space

**Jaagruk Bharat** (Delhi, founded April 2024) raised ₹1.5 crore pre-seed from AJVC in October 2025 for a government schemes access portal — proving investor appetite exists for civic tech at pre-seed. **eGov Foundation** (founded 2003 by Nandan Nilekani) has scaled its DIGIT platform to **2,600+ towns across 16 states, impacting 16 crore citizens** — proving civic infrastructure can achieve massive national scale when built as a Digital Public Good. **SocialCops** (now Atlan) built India's National Data Platform (DISHA) used by every MP and powered Ujjwala Yojana monitoring for 50M women — proving government data partnerships can be transformative. **Reap Benefit's Solve Ninja platform** has activated 121,000+ civic volunteers across 12 states with 560,000 crowdsourced data points — proving citizen-generated civic data works at scale.

International comparables strengthen the pitch further. **Pol.is** (used in Taiwan, Austria, Uruguay, and by UNDP) demonstrated that ML-powered consensus detection across divided groups can produce actual legislation. Taiwan's **vTaiwan** processed 26 national issues with 80% leading to decisive government action. Barcelona's **Decidim** platform serves 80+ governments worldwide for participatory budgeting and citizen co-governance. **Sushaasan brings this proven model to India with multilingual AI and Bharat-specific governance context at population scale.**
